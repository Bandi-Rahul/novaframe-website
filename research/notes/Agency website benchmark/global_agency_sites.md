## 1. What section order and hero patterns do the best-regarded video and social agency sites share?

### Takeaway
Across the secondhand descriptions, the dominant hero is a full-bleed autoplaying video or reel (often with little or no copy) or a single bold positioning line plus one or two sentences, followed immediately by work. Services lists come after work, not before. Spec-style agencies (small/productized) instead lead with proof numbers or packaged pricing. I could not verify the exact full section order of any single site.

### Cited Findings
- Mosaic (https://mosaic.com/): large video fills above the fold, cycling scenes (a rave, a sleek office, a failed experiment) with no copy; curated case studies follow below showing service range (pop-ups, PR stunts, influencer, AR/VR); clients cited include Budweiser and Google. Not opened by me. — [Teamwork](https://www.teamwork.com/blog/marketing-agency-websites/)
- That Lot (http://thatlot.co.uk/), a platform-first social agency: 3D background video fills the screen above the fold; mostly black-and-white palette with small bursts of pink; B&W photography throughout with colour reserved for the "Our Work" section. Not opened by me. — [Teamwork](https://www.teamwork.com/blog/marketing-agency-websites/)
- Collins (https://www.wearecollins.com/): hero is one strong headline plus two sentences explaining the offer; plain, jargon-free tone. Not opened by me. — [Teamwork](https://www.teamwork.com/blog/marketing-agency-websites/)
- 72andSunny (https://www.72andsunny.com/): hero showcases work for NFL, Amazon, United Airlines; fixed scroll moves through a mission statement on a full-frame gradient, a call for more work, culture and client-brand sections, then ways to partner. Not opened by me. — [Teamwork](https://www.teamwork.com/blog/marketing-agency-websites/)
- Madwell (https://www.madwell.com/): cycling interactive hero (including a Snake game); spare structure, reportedly no about, team or capabilities page; work itself is proof; self-deprecating copy ("a small but nimble team of weirdos"). Not opened by me. — [Teamwork](https://www.teamwork.com/blog/marketing-agency-websites/)
- Upperquad (https://upperquad.com/): full-screen high-impact video hero; animated case-study previews with generous whitespace; playful, cinematic tone. Not opened by me. — [DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/trends/top-7-advertising-agency-websites-that-demonstrate-unique-value)
- Dogstudio (https://dogstudio.co/): cinematic hero reel with WebGL animation; case studies with fluid WebGL transitions; exploratory navigation; Studio page stresses long-term partnership and transparent collaboration. Not opened by me. — [DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/trends/top-7-advertising-agency-websites-that-demonstrate-unique-value)
- Doner (https://www.doner.com/): dynamic video opening, case studies with smooth animation. DDB (https://www.ddb.com/): opens with the statement "We are the Emotional Advantage Agency." DD.NYC (https://dd.nyc/): gallery-like homepage with 3D visuals, large-format case studies with hover animation, award recognition. Not opened by me. — [DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/trends/top-7-advertising-agency-websites-that-demonstrate-unique-value)
- Instrument (https://www.instrument.com/): homepage is a scrolling portfolio stream; each entry shows client name, output type and one image. Huge (https://www.hugeinc.com/): leads with provocative positioning rather than a services list, case studies framed around business outcomes, dark palette. Not opened by me. — [KrishaWeb](https://www.krishaweb.com/blog/best-creative-agency-websites/)

Grow Pronto: opens with ecommerce proof (revenue influenced, brands served, review scores) and a free audit CTA. Bolder: headline states audience and problem ("We brand the companies that are hardest to explain."). ### Inferences
- A video-first agency whose hero shows no real footage is unusual among these examples; the commonly cited hero devices are real video (Mosaic, Upperquad, Dogstudio, Doner, That Lot) or a bold statement (Collins, DDB, Bolder).
- Typical order implied: hero (video or statement) -> selected work -> services/capabilities -> proof (logos/clients/awards) -> about/culture -> contact/footer. This is my synthesis from several partial descriptions, not an observed order on one site.
- Novaframe's hero (statement headline, lede, two CTAs, three animated phone frames) fits the "statement + CTA" archetype, but its visuals are illustrations, not footage.

## 2. How do top agencies show work and proof, and which patterns work with little or no portfolio?

### Takeaway
Leaders let the work carry proof (Madwell, Dirtverse, Instrument), supplemented by named client lists, award mentions and quotes. For thin portfolios, a conversion write-up advises a few sharp, structured case studies (or one or two flagship pieces) over a large gallery, and making structure do the work. Productized small agencies show proof through pricing, timelines and policies.

### Cited Findings
- Small set of focused examples can beat a large unfocused gallery; use a case-study-led homepage featuring one or two flagship projects; say who you help best so low volume does not leave the offer vague. — [Unicorn Platform](https://unicornplatform.com/blog/agency-portfolio-sites-in-2026/)
- Repeatable case-study structure: client context, challenge, approach, selected work, outcome, next step; show the problem before the visuals; make some change visible without needing inflated numbers. — [Unicorn Platform](https://unicornplatform.com/blog/agency-portfolio-sites-in-2026/)
- Area 17 (https://area17.com/): candid case studies covering brief, process and constraints; a thinking section doubles as content hub. Ueno (https://ueno.co/): case studies show the thinking behind work. Wolff Olins (https://www.wolffolins.com/): small set of high-profile cases, not a full archive. (Secondhand.) — [KrishaWeb](https://www.krishaweb.com/blog/best-creative-agency-websites/)
- Proof types seen in the secondhand descriptions: named clients (Mosaic: Budweiser, Google), performance metrics and creator network showcase (Ubiquitous, https://www.ubiquitousinfluence.com/), prominent metrics and outcomes (HigherVisibility, https://www.highervisibility.com/), risk-reversal guarantee plus shared audit findings (Disruptive Advertising, https://disruptiveadvertising.com/), animated charts and data points (KlientBoost, https://www.klientboost.com/), awards (DD.NYC). — [Teamwork](https://www.teamwork.com/blog/marketing-agency-websites/), [DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/trends/top-7-advertising-agency-websites-that-demonstrate-unique-value)
- Productized small agencies: Bureau Dimanche shows packaged services with prices, timelines and deliverables before a booking call, plus recent work and client quotes; Default Studio shows pricing, timelines, revision policies and handover details; Huehaus shows starting prices and delivery ranges; Another Agency shows social media packages with prices. (URLs not given in source.) — [Framer blog](https://www.framer.com/blog/marketing-agency-websites/)
- Social/video agency listing: most listings use named client logos (Genero: Adidas, Microsoft, Toyota, Netflix, IKEA; Whalar: Estee Lauder, Google, Vodafone; Viral Nation: Coca-Cola, Disney, Microsoft; Fresh Content Society "since 2014"). The same page notes it found no case-study metrics for any agency and that many client claims are generic placeholders and unverifiable. Entry pricing like "from $3,000/mo" to "$5,000/mo" is common on a third-party listing (not agency sites). — [Web Tonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/best-social-video-agencies)

### Inferences
- Novaframe's honest "no real client work yet" position matches the advice to be structured rather than inflated; its own-Reel storyboard is a creative stand-in for a case study but is a script, not footage, so it proves thinking, not output.
- Pricing-forward proof (Bureau Dimanche, Default Studio, Huehaus) is the closest precedent for Novaframe's from-INR package cards.

### Gaps
- No verified testimonial placement/format data from live sites.
- No source on how agencies with zero portfolio show proof beyond the Unicorn Platform advice; no named example of a newly launched video agency site found.

## 3. How do the best sites present teams, and what makes them feel human?

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin. Some leaders (Madwell) skip a team page and let personality in copy and imagery carry it; Beans uses fun photography and props; Ueno and Pentagram make people central. 2026 design commentary says generic stock is "invisible at best and trust-undermining" and overpolished sites read as untrustworthy. I found no source describing horizontal team scrollers or carousels.

### Cited Findings
- Beans Agency (https://beans.agency/): fun photography and props show the small staff; humorous, playful tone; infinite-scroll navigation (described as debated). Not opened by me. — [Teamwork](https://www.teamwork.com/blog/marketing-agency-websites/)
- Madwell: reportedly no team page; people shown through imagery. — [Teamwork](https://www.teamwork.com/blog/marketing-agency-websites/)
- Ueno: team page presents people as central; Pentagram (https://www.pentagram.com/): partner model central, names individual creative directors. — [KrishaWeb](https://www.krishaweb.com/blog/best-creative-agency-websites/)
- Bureau Dimanche: founder's CMO and creative-director background highlighted. Another Agency: team leadership featured. — [Framer blog](https://www.framer.com/blog/marketing-agency-websites/)
- 2026 trend commentary: retire generic stock (handshakes, smiling at laptops); avoid AI illustrations with flat lighting and uniform glows; visible human craft and hand-drawn details create authenticity; "overpolishing backfires" with younger audiences. The source gives no specific team-photo guidance. — [DesignRush trends](https://www.designrush.com/agency/website-design-development/trends/web-design-trends)

### Inferences
- Novaframe's monogram cards (J, H, D, R) are the opposite of the "real faces/props" approach; with no photos they weaken the "four friends" human claim. Real photos or short looping clips of each person on a shoot would be the closest fit to what the sources praise. (Inference; no source directly tests monograms.)

### Gaps
- Nothing found on horizontal scrollers/carousels for team sections; cannot confirm that pattern's prevalence.

## 4. Typical CTA placement and wording, and conversion elements above the fold

### Takeaway
Advice favors one primary CTA for ready buyers and one secondary for proof-seekers, placed after enough context; avoid "Let's talk" as the only action. Observed wording in sources: booking link, free audit, risk-reversal guarantee, prices before booking.

### Cited Findings
- Primary: "Book a discovery call" / "Start a project conversation"; secondary: "See selected case studies" / "Explore our approach"; avoid vague "Let's talk" alone. — [Unicorn Platform](https://unicornplatform.com/blog/agency-portfolio-sites-in-2026/)
- Grow Pronto: free audit CTA with proof stats in hero. Metasense: booking link. Disruptive Advertising: prominent guarantee. KlientBoost: bold prospect-facing CTA. — [Framer blog](https://www.framer.com/blog/marketing-agency-websites/), [DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/trends/top-7-advertising-agency-websites-that-demonstrate-unique-value)
- Nav pattern notes: Made by Many nav moves visitors from case studies to services; KlientBoost nav organized around business goals; Dogstudio menu "toggles subtly"; Dillinger.tv uses animated director names and project titles as primary navigation. — [DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/trends/top-7-advertising-agency-websites-that-demonstrate-unique-value), [DesignRush trends](https://www.designrush.com/agency/website-design-development/trends/web-design-trends)

### Inferences
- Novaframe already has a strong low-friction offer ("free sample Reel", repeated in header and hero) and a reassurance line ("No obligation"), which aligns with free-audit style CTAs. Its secondary CTA "See packages" leads to prices rather than proof, which is the weaker of the two advised secondary types.

### Gaps
- No verified data (A/B or conversion rates) on CTA wording for video agencies; no footer-content source found for any agency.

## 5. Typography, motion, and tone trends

### Takeaway
2026 commentary stresses expressive/kinetic typography, restrained purposeful motion, performance-first video, and human-made imperfection.

### Cited Findings
- Kinetic type is "the primary carrier of personality"; entrance animations 300-600ms, stagger 50-100ms; liquid loops 6-10s; respect reduced-motion; use Lottie/CSS instead of GIF/video loops; replace autoplay hero video with a poster image that upgrades to compressed video once interactive. — [DesignRush trends](https://www.designrush.com/agency/website-design-development/trends/web-design-trends)
- Tone examples: playful/self-deprecating (Madwell, Beans), plain/direct (Collins), data-driven (HigherVisibility, Ubiquitous uses CPC/CAC jargon), bold/authoritative (Disruptive), friendly/approachable (Koto, https://koto.studio/). — [Teamwork](https://www.teamwork.com/blog/marketing-agency-websites/), [KrishaWeb](https://www.krishaweb.com/blog/best-creative-agency-websites/), [DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/trends/top-7-advertising-agency-websites-that-demonstrate-unique-value)
- Expressive type gives RabenRifaie a recognizable identity; Another Agency uses oversized type; Twoplusone uses playful typography. (URLs not given.) — [Framer blog](https://www.framer.com/blog/marketing-agency-websites/)

### Inferences
- Novaframe's Bricolage Grotesque display face, mono timecodes and CSS-only phone-bar animation are consistent with expressive-type plus lightweight-motion advice and honor reduced-motion thinking only partly (its file has no prefers-reduced-motion rule; I saw none in the CSS I read).

## 6. What does Novaframe lack relative to these leaders?

### Takeaway
Based on what I read in novaframe.html versus the secondhand patterns: real moving footage/showreel in the hero, any named client or work proof, testimonials, real faces, a visible work/portfolio section (its storyboard is a script), FAQ, a proper footer (contact, socials, legal), and a proof-oriented secondary CTA. All of these are comparisons against secondhand descriptions, not verified leader pages.

### Cited Findings
- Novaframe file (read directly): no testimonials, no case studies, no real video, no FAQ, no client logos; contact channels are config placeholders showing "Coming soon" for WhatsApp, Instagram and email; footer is a logo and one line; team cards are monograms. — Local file novaframe.html (path above)
- Leaders' above-the-fold elements per advice: positioning line, supporting sentence, primary CTA, proof cue. — [Unicorn Platform](https://unicornplatform.com/blog/agency-portfolio-sites-in-2026/)
- Honest offsets Novaframe already has that sources praise: plain tone, pricing/process transparency (Default Studio, Bureau Dimanche pattern), a launch offer trading discount for testimonial/portfolio permission (not described in any source; this is Novaframe's own tactic).

### Inferences
- Highest-leverage fixes: (1) put real motion in the hero (a real 9:16 clip of the team, even self-made); (2) replace monograms with real photos; (3) add a "work" section even if it is 1-2 real sample Reels made for local shops, framed with problem/approach/outcome; (4) change the secondary CTA to a proof path ("Watch our intro Reel"); (5) fill contact channels, because "Coming soon" on every channel undercuts the CTA; (6) add a short FAQ and a fuller footer.
- Caution: the claim that leaders all have footers with specific contents is unsupported by what I could open.

- Could not verify any footer contents of any agency site.
- A browser-enabled follow-up (or allowlisting agency domains) is needed to actually open at least 8 live sites; the current "named sites with URLs" list (Mosaic, That Lot, Collins, 72andSunny, Madwell, Beans, Ubiquitous, Upperquad, Dogstudio, DD.NYC, Instrument, Huge, Hello Monday, B-Reel, KlientBoost, Made by Many, Disruptive Advertising, HigherVisibility, Koto, Area 17) is secondhand only.
