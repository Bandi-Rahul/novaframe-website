# Novaframe trails market on proof, not honesty

Novaframe is a clean, honest, well-built single page that matches or beats the market on tone, pricing transparency, process clarity and named people, but it falls short of market standard on the things a video agency is judged by: **real footage, any proof of output, a working contact path, and baseline SEO**. The page shows no real video (its hero is three CSS-drawn phone frames and its "first Reel" is a script), no client, testimonial or review, and all three contact channels read "Coming soon" because the `CONFIG` values are empty, so the form can only compose text for the visitor to copy. Its pricing display (three "from" tiers in INR), five-step monthly process and four named team members are at or above what the sampled Indian agencies offer. The comparison against global leaders rests entirely on secondhand editorial descriptions, because no agency homepage could be opened in this session. The Indian comparison rests on 11 sites read through summarising fetches, and the conversion and technical findings rest on a mix of primary standards (Google, W3C, NN/g) and weaker vendor claims. The recommended changes split cleanly: roughly a dozen can be done inside the HTML now (title and description, mobile navigation, reduced-motion, contrast, FAQ, footer, CTA wording, a WhatsApp-first path ready to switch on), and five need input from the four founders (real contact details, photos, footage, a first named client, and business details).

## What the evidence can and cannot support

The notes are explicit that **no agency homepage was viewed directly** for the global set. Fetches of wearesocial.com, fanbytes.co.uk, lemonlight.com, vidico.com, mosaic.com, madwell.com and others were blocked, so every global observation (Mosaic's wordless full-screen video, Collins's one-headline-plus-two-sentences hero, Madwell's missing team page, Bureau Dimanche's priced packages) comes from listicles by Teamwork, KrishaWeb, DesignRush and the Framer blog, and some of those articles themselves did not visit the sites. I treat them as editorial claims. The same applies to the layout order of any named site: the "hero, work, services, proof, about, contact" sequence is my synthesis from partial descriptions, not an observed order on any one site. One listing, Web Tonic, is a self-ranking publisher with placeholder text and low reliability, and I give it no weight.

The Indian set is stronger but still indirect. Eleven sites were opened (Webhopers, upGrowth, SocioLabs, VK Digital Solution, Mayank Digital Labs, Balistro, Noir & Blanco, W3era, Wildnet, WeblinkIndia, IRPR), but through a tool that returns a model-written summary, not raw HTML or screenshots, so wording is reliable only where quoted and **visual design, sticky behaviour and mobile layout were not assessed**. Several were sub-pages (pricing or service pages) rather than homepages, the sample skews to SEO and performance agencies in Delhi NCR, Mohali and Pune, and large networks such as Social Beat and Kinnect were not openable. No small, video-first, on-site-shooting studio was found in the sample, so there is no like-for-like peer. Read the Indian results as "what 11 reachable sites do", not a market census.

Novaframe itself was read directly from source, so statements about its content, markup and CSS are observation, not inference. What was not done: no rendering, no Lighthouse or Core Web Vitals run, no screen-reader or real-device test, and no check of what the host injects into the head. Contrast figures below are my own arithmetic from the CSS tokens, not tool output. Evidence on conversion is mostly qualitative (NN/g) or vendor-reported with no sample sizes, and no study specific to new agencies, India or local-business video agencies was found. So the report ranks changes by how well they fit the primary guidance and the observed gaps, not by measured lift.

## Hero, structure and proof: strong copy, no evidence of output

The homepage runs hero, three promises, packages, process, storyboard, team, contact, footer. Secondhand descriptions of global leaders put work first and services after: Mosaic, Upperquad, Dogstudio and That Lot reportedly open on full-screen video, while Collins, DDB and Bolder open on a bold statement ([Teamwork](https://www.teamwork.com/blog/marketing-agency-websites/), [DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/trends/top-7-advertising-agency-websites-that-demonstrate-unique-value)). Novaframe sits in the second camp: "Your shop is great. Let's make sure people see it." is a plain, audience-specific statement hero with a lede, two CTAs and a no-obligation line. A conversion write-up recommends exactly that stack of one positioning line, one supporting sentence, one primary CTA and one proof cue ([Unicorn Platform](https://unicornplatform.com/blog/agency-portfolio-sites-in-2026/)). Novaframe has the first four and lacks the proof cue. Its tone is also warmer than the Indian sample, where superlatives and "we don't sell cheap packages" copy dominate ([SocioLabs](https://sociolabs.in/social-media-marketing-packages-pricing-india/)).

The weakness is that a video-first agency shows no video. The hero frames are `aria-hidden` illustrations with a looping progress bar, and the "Our first Reel" section is a six-beat script for a Reel that does not exist yet. NN/g found users want to see the real work and who does it, and discount on-site testimonials as cherry-picked, trusting external reviews more ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)). Among Indian peers, the credible ones stack a Google rating with review count, a logo strip and two or three outcomes in business terms, for example Webhopers' "531 Business Calls" and 4.8 from 453 reviews ([Webhopers](https://www.webhopers.com/)); even the tiny Reels shop VK Digital leads with a single hard result, "2.1M views on a single Reel" ([VK Digital Solution](https://vkdigitalsolution.com/reels-editing-services/)). Those same sites also show how proof backfires: conflicting client counts on Webhopers, Wildnet and VK, and visible template errors on Wildnet. Novaframe's decision to claim nothing it cannot back avoids that trap, and "We can't promise followers or sales" is rare in the sample (only Wildnet carries a comparable line, per [Wildnet](https://wildnettechnologies.com/digital-marketing/local-seo-services)). The right move for a zero-portfolio agency is not invented numbers but a small number of structured, real pieces: advice for thin portfolios is one or two flagship case studies with client context, challenge, approach and outcome rather than a large gallery ([Unicorn Platform](https://unicornplatform.com/blog/agency-portfolio-sites-in-2026/)). The free sample Reel is the best trust device Novaframe has, since it lets a prospect judge work on their own shop; it is a close match for VK's "Free Sample Edit" and stronger than the generic "free consultation" most peers use.

## Pricing, process and team: where Novaframe already meets the standard

Pricing display is at market standard on format and above it on clarity. "Starting at ₹X / month" with named tiers is the dominant format among the four Indian sites that publish prices, and the best of them add a scope matrix and minimum term ([Webhopers](https://www.webhopers.com/pricing/social-media-marketing-packages)). Novaframe's ₹8,000 for 8 videos, ₹18,000 for 12 videos plus basic Meta ads management, and ₹12,000 for a 4 to 5 page site sit between VK's editing-only ₹6,000 for 8 Reels and agency floors of ₹12,000 to ₹15,000 for social packages, and inside Hostinger India's ₹10,000 to ₹80,000 range for a small business site ([Hostinger India](https://www.hostinger.com/in/tutorials/website-cost)). Because Novaframe includes shooting while VK sells editing only, the price is competitive. Disclosure matches NN/g's call to state costs up front ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/communicating-trustworthiness/)), and the separate-ad-budget note matches the market. What is missing is what the market's better sites also state: what moves a "from" price, the minimum term or no-lock-in, whether GST is included (none of the opened sites said, so this is an open question rather than a gap against peers), and what is excluded such as travel, music licensing and extra shoot days. One data point to be cautious with: the claim that published prices produce better-qualified but fewer leads comes from an unsourced agency blog ([Eight25Media](https://www.eight25media.com/blog/transparent-vs-non-transparent-pricing-the-real-impact-on-sales)), so treat it as plausible, not established.

The process section is a real strength. A before-month plan, shoot, edit, revise, report sequence with named people and a 48-hour feedback ask is the "show the process" pattern NN/g recommends, and the batch-shoot "one visit a month" framing answers the owner's biggest worry, time. I found no sampled Indian site with a comparably concrete month. The team section is similarly ahead of the market: Webhopers, SocioLabs and Noir & Blanco link a team page with no individual profiles, and Madwell reportedly skips team pages altogether. Novaframe names four people, with roles and a first-point-of-contact line. Its build is also good: native buttons of 48 px (above WCAG 2.5.8's 24 px minimum, per [W3C](https://w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)), a labelled focusable region, scroll-snap, `overscroll-behavior-x: contain`, and a motion-aware `scrollBy`. That already satisfies most of the dragging-alternative and carousel guidance in the [W3C APG pattern](https://w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/carousel). The weakness is that the faces are letter monograms (J, H, D, R), which undercuts the "four friends" claim; sources praise real photography and props (Beans) and warn that generic or synthetic imagery reads as untrustworthy ([DesignRush trends](https://www.designrush.com/agency/website-design-development/trends/web-design-trends)). No source tests monograms directly, so that judgement is mine.

## Conversion path is the most urgent defect

This is the one area where the page is broken for its stated purpose, not merely below benchmark. All three CTAs point to `#contact`, where the form does not submit anywhere: it composes a message the visitor must copy, and the "Open WhatsApp" link stays hidden while `CONFIG.whatsapp` is empty. WhatsApp, Instagram and email rows all show "Coming soon". Nearly every sampled Indian site offers a tap-to-chat WhatsApp link with prefilled text ("Hi Balistro, I'd like a free growth audit"), a phone number, and often a response promise such as "within 2 hours" ([Balistro](https://www.balistro.com/blog/digital-marketing-strategies-small-businesses-india), [Mayank Digital Labs](https://www.mayankdigitallabs.in/ai-reels-video-editing-delhi)). The only quantified evidence that WhatsApp beats forms (18 to 35% click rates against 6 to 12%) comes from a Mumbai agency that sells WhatsApp funnels, with no methodology ([Prateeksha](https://prateeksha.com/blog/whatsapp-vs-contact-forms-enquiries-indian-business)), so I treat it as directional only; the stronger argument is simply that every extra step and every dead channel is friction, and NN/g found hidden or hard-to-reach contact details drive users away ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)).

Secondary issues compound it. The hero's second CTA, "See packages", sends a proof-seeker to prices, not to proof; the advised secondary is a proof path such as "See selected case studies" ([Unicorn Platform](https://unicornplatform.com/blog/agency-portfolio-sites-in-2026/)). CTA wording is inconsistent ("Get a free sample", "Get a free sample Reel", "Prepare my message"), and the form button loses the promise at the moment of commitment; a Conversion Rate Experts test saw about a 29% lift from placing a guarantee on the button, but it is a single self-reported test ([Conversion Rate Experts](https://conversion-rate-experts.com/button-microcopy-win-report/)), so I use it only as support for putting the reassurance line next to the button. On validation, empty required fields only move focus: the code clears the status text and gives no error message, which fails the plain expectation that a user be told what is wrong. There is no FAQ, no response-time promise, no phone number or hours, no registered business detail, and the footer is a logo and a copyright line. I found no source showing what leaders put in footers, so footer content rests on peer practice (WeblinkIndia lists ISO and a company name, Noir & Blanco a private limited name) rather than evidence. Finally, the video question: Unbounce's analysis of about 35,000 pages per period found video pages convert about the same or slightly worse than no-video pages ([Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/landing-page-optimization/video-on-landing-pages)), so a real clip should be click-to-play, captioned and A/B tested, not assumed to lift conversion. The case for it is credibility for a video agency, not a measured conversion gain.

## Mobile, performance, accessibility and SEO

Roughly 81% of professional-services landing visits are mobile ([Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/professional-services-conversion-rate/)), and the layout is mostly built for it: fluid grids with `auto-fit`, `clamp()` type, safe-area padding, 16 px gutters and 48 px rail buttons. The notable mobile defect is that **below 820 px the whole nav is `display:none` with no menu replacement**, so phone visitors see only the logo and the "Get a free sample" button and cannot jump to packages, process or team. Peers use a persistent WhatsApp or call button on mobile; Novaframe has no sticky tap-to-chat or `tel:` link, though I could not verify sticky behaviour on any peer because the fetch summaries do not capture it.

On performance, the thresholds are LCP at or under 2.5 s, INP at or under 200 ms and CLS at or under 0.1 at the 75th percentile ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds)). The page has no images or video, which helps, but it loads three Google Font families (Bricolage Grotesque in two weights, DM Sans in three, JetBrains Mono in one) through a render-blocking stylesheet with `display=swap`. With text as the likely LCP element, font loading is the main risk, and web.dev's guidance is fewer weights, matched fallback metrics or `font-display: optional` ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-cls)). The hero bar animation uses `transform`, which is the layout-safe pattern. This is inference from source; no measurement was made. Any future video needs a `poster`, reserved aspect ratio and `preload="none"` and must not become the LCP element ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/lazy-loading-video)).

Accessibility is partly good and partly unverified. Good: visible `for`-associated labels, `autocomplete` values, a focus-visible style, 2px outlines, an `h1`, a labelled `nav`, `aria-live` status text and a theme-aware palette. Problems I can state from the CSS: there is no `prefers-reduced-motion` rule, so the three infinitely looping hero bars and `html{scroll-behavior:smooth}` ignore the user setting (the JS rail does honour it); input borders and ghost-button borders use `--line` (#D6DBE5 on white, roughly 1.4:1 by my calculation, and similarly low in dark mode), which is below the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for where a border is the only identifier of a field ([W3C](https://w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast)); and small 10 to 12 px mono labels carry meaning. Muted text on the paper background clears 4.5:1 by my estimate, but a full both-theme contrast pass was not run. One correction to the technical notes: they list the team scroller as needing buttons, but it already has them.

SEO basics are the cheapest gap to close. The `<title>` is just "Novaframe", which Google treats as a half-empty title it may rewrite, and there is no meta description, canonical, Open Graph, favicon link or JSON-LD in the file ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link), [Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet)). Google sets no length limit, so the often-quoted 60 and 160 character rules are display heuristics, not rules. Organization structured data has no required properties, but name, url, logo and sameAs are recommended, and LocalBusiness needs a real address ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/logo), [Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business)). Nothing says where Novaframe operates, so a local-service agency gives no city or service-area signal. It is not known whether the host strips head tags, sets `lang`, or injects a canonical, so each change must be checked against the live source. I found no source on Google Business Profile weighting; its importance for local discovery is practitioner judgement.

## Prioritised changes

**Fix now (can be done in the page itself)**

1. Rewrite the title and add a description and Organization JSON-LD. Use brand, service and place (for example "Novaframe | Video marketing and websites for local businesses in [city]") and a one-sentence description led by the free sample; add a favicon link and Open Graph tags, then check the live source to see what the host keeps.
2. Add a mobile menu or an in-page link row. Replace `display:none` on the nav under 820 px with a disclosure menu or a compact anchor row, keeping targets at least 44 px.
3. Make the contact path work as soon as a number exists. Wire the WhatsApp deep link with prefilled text as the primary button, keep the form as secondary, and add a sticky mobile "WhatsApp us" and call bar once values are set. Until then, replace "Coming soon" with a visible fallback (an Instagram DM or a mailto form) so the page is never a dead end.
4. Fix form behaviour and wording. Show visible, associated error messages on empty required fields (`aria-describedby`), add a phone or WhatsApp number field, and rename the button to restate the promise ("Request my free sample Reel"); use one consistent CTA phrase everywhere.
5. Change the hero secondary CTA to a proof path ("See how we'd film your shop", linking to the storyboard) until real work exists, and put a short reassurance line next to each primary CTA ("No obligation, you approve scripts before we film, cancel any month" only if the team confirms it).
6. Add `prefers-reduced-motion` guards for the infinite bar animation and `scroll-behavior:smooth`.
7. Raise border contrast to at least 3:1 for inputs, ghost buttons and the rail buttons in both themes, and run a full contrast check of every token pair.
8. Add an FAQ and a fuller footer. Cover the sample (what, turnaround, who keeps it), what "from" means, GST and minimum term (as placeholders to confirm), who owns footage and accounts, revisions, shoot-day logistics, ad-spend handling and languages. The footer should carry the contact rows, a short "who we are" line, and legal and business details when known.
9. State what moves each price and what is excluded under the package cards, and add a one-line "what you get in the first 30 days" under the process.
10. Trim fonts (drop weights, add fallback metric overrides or `font-display: optional`) and measure LCP with a throttled mobile Lighthouse run.
11. Add a Hindi or Hinglish line near the hero CTA and, once confirmed, state the cities served. No sampled site had a full regional-language version, so this would be a differentiator, not catch-up.
12. Reframe the storyboard honestly as "The script for our first Reel" with a "Coming soon: watch it here" slot designed to take a real video, so the section is ready the day footage exists.

**Needs input from the team**

1. Real contact details: a WhatsApp number, Instagram handle, email, a phone number with hours and the response time the team can actually keep.
2. Real photos or short clips of Joshua, Hemanth, Dhathri and Rahul on a shoot, replacing the monograms, with consent to publish.
3. Real footage: a 20 to 30 second captioned vertical Reel (the team's own intro Reel is the obvious first one) for a click-to-play hero or work section, then one or two sample Reels made for friendly local shops.
4. A first named client and proof: a case study with problem, approach and outcome in terms owners value (calls, walk-ins, enquiries), with consent, and a request that pilot clients post a Google Business Profile review, since NN/g found external reviews are trusted more than on-site quotes ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)).
5. Business and policy facts: city or service area, a registered name or address, GST and Udyam details if any, the actual minimum term and refund or cancellation terms, footage and account ownership, the real number of free-sample slots per week, and confirmation of GST treatment on prices. Also a Google Business Profile for Novaframe itself.

## Conclusion

The finding that changes the picture is that Novaframe's weaknesses are mostly absences, not mistakes. Its honesty is an asset in a market where the reachable Indian agency sites contradict their own numbers; what it lacks is anything a skeptical shop owner can verify. Because the benchmark sites were not observed directly, the sharpest claim is a conditional one: the specific elements named above are absent from Novaframe by direct reading, and every statement about what leaders do rests on editorial description or on a small, uneven sample.

The practical implication is that the fastest gain is sequencing, not redesign. The in-page fixes make the page discoverable, usable on a phone and ready to convert; the team inputs (a working WhatsApp number, one real Reel and one named client) convert the page from a credible script into verifiable proof. A browser-enabled follow-up that opens at least eight live agency homepages and runs Lighthouse on Novaframe would turn the largest remaining inferences into observations.
