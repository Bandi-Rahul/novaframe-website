# Novaframe must prove its work, not just promise

Novaframe's page already does what most Indian agency sites do not: it shows a starting price (₹8,000 a month), a concrete process, named founders and a Telugu-aware voice. To reach industry standard it now needs four things: **a contact path that opens WhatsApp without friction, verifiable proof of work, crawlable Telugu and English content, and a fast, accessible mobile build.** The strongest evidence supports the mechanics: Google's own documentation on language URLs, structured data and Core Web Vitals, W3C's WCAG 2.2, and Nielsen Norman Group (NN/g) guidance on showing prices. The weakest evidence covers exactly what Novaframe most needs to decide: whether WhatsApp beats a form in India, whether founder photos build trust for a brand-new agency, and how Telugu and Tinglish visitors search. Nobody has measured these. The notes also flag that no local Vizag, Vijayawada or Hyderabad agency homepage was opened directly, so "the market standard" here means a sample of about a dozen pages read through summaries. I have not seen the current Novaframe page; its features come from your description. Most in-page fixes are cheap and well supported. The changes that would most improve credibility (real footage, real faces, a real number) depend on the team and cannot be shipped from the page alone.

## The single-page language switch trades search reach for simplicity

Your design switches language only on the Reel script, and that is a sound choice for readability. It is also the decision with the largest search consequence. Google recommends **a separate URL per language version, linked with reciprocal hreflang tags**, and warns that content swapped by a cookie or browser setting may not be found and crawled, because Googlebot usually crawls from the USA and sends no Accept-Language header ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites)). Fully translated versions are not treated as duplicates ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions)). The same page says that translating only boilerplate around untranslated main content gives a poor result, and that Google reads the page language from visible content, not from `lang` attributes. If most of the page is English and only the Reel script toggles, then Telugu is a feature of the page, not an indexed offering. The page will rank, if at all, for English queries only.

Whether that matters depends on how Novaframe's customers search, and the notes cannot say. IAMAI-Kantar found 98% of Indian internet users accessed Indic-language content in 2024 and that Telugu is among the most popular Indic languages, but gave **no Telugu-specific user count and no Tinglish query share** ([The Week/PTI](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/01/16/dcm51-biz-iamai-internet-usage-report.html)). Google has said that typing in Indian scripts is hard, so many people search in English even when they prefer local-language results ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/12/16/google-expands-languages-push-in-india-to-serve-non-english-speakers/amp)). The reasonable inference is that English and Latin-script queries carry most commercial search intent, while Telugu serves trust, voice and WhatsApp conversation. That is an inference, not a measurement. It suggests a proportionate path: keep the single page, but make the Telugu layer visible beyond the Reel script (headline, CTA, FAQ answers), keep one script per text unit with brand names as exceptions, and write colloquial Telugu with English loanwords for technical terms, with numerals in English and prices in ₹ ([Think with Google](https://thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/13326/App_and_website_localization_for_Indian_users.pdf)). A native reviewer should check the Telugu; the notes give no dialect guidance, so regional vocabulary differences between Telangana and coastal Andhra stay an uncited risk. Real `/te/` and `/en/` URLs with hreflang are the standard answer if Telugu search becomes a goal, but that is a later step, and Search Console query data should justify it first.

Two related points need care. Google's spam policy targets scaled translated pages made to manipulate rankings, so a handful of city landing pages must carry real content ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)). And on fonts: Noto Sans Telugu is a variable font whose uncompressed TTF is **868 KB** ([Fontsource](https://fontsource.org/fonts/noto-sans-telugu/about)), so it should be self-hosted as WOFF2, subset by Unicode block (not by the characters on the page, which risks breaking conjuncts), and limited to two weights ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/reduce-webfont-size)). The notes could not measure real WOFF2 sizes, so measure before committing.

## Proof is the standard, and a new agency can supply it honestly

The earlier benchmark found that Novaframe's honesty is an asset: reachable Indian agency sites contradict their own client counts, show round unsourced stats ("150+ businesses served") or cite partner badges the page does not verify ([OwlClaw](https://owlclaw.com/locations/visakhapatnam-digital-marketing-agency)). Newfangled's recommended order is positioning, then one or two matching case studies, then client quotes tied to them ([Newfangled](https://www.newfangled.com/how-to-improve-your-agency-website-home-page-design/)). For a video agency this means footage near the top. The one source on whether video lifts conversion says pages with video convert about the same or slightly worse, so the argument for a real clip is credibility, not measured lift, and it should be click-to-play and captioned ([Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/landing-page-optimization/video-on-landing-pages)).

With no clients, the honest substitutes are specific. Show your own intro Reel as the first piece of work, and label any sample made for a friendly or fictional business as a sample. Offer a free or paid entry product: a Vizag agency sells a ₹499 strategy call and ₹1,999 SEO audit ([Mayank Digital Labs](https://www.mayankdigitallabs.in/blog/digital-marketing-agency-visakhapatnam-2026)), though that agency's own page also advertises a "free 30 minute strategy call", so even the sampled example is inconsistent. State a revision policy and what the client owns. Never show invented testimonials, logos or countdowns: India's 2023 dark-pattern guidelines list 13 prohibited patterns, though the notes only saw the headline list and not the text ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/govt-issues-list-of-13-dark-patterns-plaguing-ecommerce-websites)). Any scarcity line, such as "limited free samples this month", must reflect a real cap.

The evidence on founder photos conflicts. A 2014 BrightLocal survey found a genuine owner photo was picked as most trustworthy by 46% against 33% for a generic photo ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/images-inspire-trust-local-business-website/)), but a 2016 CXL study found versions without the human picture scored higher on professionalism on all eight agency homepages, significant on three, and its author warns the cause may be simply fewer page elements ([CXL](https://cxl.com/research-study/appeal-to-authority-credibility/)). Both are old and US-based. The conclusion is modest: real photos belong in the team section with name, role and a direct contact link, but they should not be expected to lift conversion by themselves, and stock people should be avoided. For a four-person team this is also checkable and cheap: small teams are credible on directories, where Hyderabad agencies of 2 to 9 people carry Clutch badges ([Clutch](https://clutch.co/in/agencies/digital-marketing/small-business/hyderabad)). Pairing this with a Google Business Profile with reviews is plausible, but the notes found no source on how much it weighs.

Pricing is a point of strength to protect. NN/g found that users leave sites without prices and shortlist competitors that show them, and recommends sample prices for typical scenarios over configurators ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/show-price/), [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/show-prices-for-common-scenarios/)). Indian agencies rarely publish rate cards, and one 2026 guide places reel production in a ₹30,000 to ₹50,000 monthly band ([Brainguru](https://blog.brainguru.in/2026/05/digital-marketing-agency-pricing-india/)). Novaframe's ₹8,000 start therefore sits far below the band quoted by that agency-authored blog. The blog has no stated methodology, and the notes found no evidence on whether a low price reads as low quality, so the page should simply state what each package includes (videos, shoot visits, revisions, turnaround), what changes the price, and whether GST is included. The notes found no source showing that Indian agencies display GST numbers, so that item is a judgment call, not a norm.

## The contact path must be one tap, small and honest

A WhatsApp-composer form is a reasonable fit for India: Meta India's head has called small businesses leading adopters of WhatsApp business messaging ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/business-messaging-whatsapp-engines-of-growth-biz-digitisation-driving-headroom-meta-india-head)), and an adjacent Vizag agency treats WhatsApp as its primary sales channel with a 24-hour reply promise ([Mayank Digital Labs](https://www.mayankdigitallabs.in/blog/digital-marketing-agency-visakhapatnam-2026)). But **no primary study measures click-to-chat conversion in India**, and the figures circulating (78% of SMBs, 65% sales lift) are labelled unverified by the page that carries them ([Hyperleap](https://hyperleap.ai/blog/whatsapp-statistics-india-2026)). Treat WhatsApp-first as a sound usage-based design choice, not a proven multiplier.

What is well supported is minimising fields: Baymard's checkout research shows field count affects usability more than step count, and the average checkout has 11.3 fields against 8 that most sites need ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/checkout-flow-average-form-fields)). That is e-commerce evidence, so a composer of three fields or fewer (name, business type, one free-text line) is an extrapolation, but a safe one. The sticky bottom CTA has only a single self-published agency test behind it, about +25% sales with no sample size ([Conversion Rate Experts](https://conversion-rate-experts.com/sticky-cta-win-report/)), though it favoured a low-commitment, outcome-framed label. A sticky bar carries two real costs: WCAG 2.2's Focus Not Obscured criterion means a keyboard-focused element cannot be fully hidden behind it, so add `scroll-padding-bottom` equal to the bar height, and overlays and third-party scripts are named causes of poor mobile INP ([W3C WAI](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/), [SpeedCurve](https://speedcurve.com/blog/core-web-vitals-inp-mobile/)). A plain `wa.me` link has no script cost. The notes could not open WhatsApp's own documentation, so the link format (`https://wa.me/<country code><number>?text=<encoded>`) is from general knowledge and should be tested on real phones.

Performance and accessibility have firm numbers. The Core Web Vitals "good" thresholds are LCP 2.5 s, INP 200 ms and CLS 0.1 at the 75th percentile ([web.dev](https://web.dev/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds/)); only **48% of mobile sites pass all three**, and the failures are unsized images, lazy-loaded LCP images and unpreloaded fonts ([HTTP Archive Web Almanac](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/performance)). For a video agency the practical rules are a poster image, a reserved aspect ratio, and a facade in place of a full YouTube embed, which can cut about 540 KB to about 3 KB until clicked ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/performance/third-party-facades?hl=en)). On accessibility, WCAG 2.2 sets a 24 px minimum target size, and 44 to 48 px is a platform-guidance design choice for primary taps ([W3C WAI](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/)). Low contrast is the most common failure on 79.1% of home pages, and missing form labels and empty buttons follow ([WebAIM](https://webaim.org/projects/million/2025)), so icon-only WhatsApp buttons need accessible names and any text over video needs a contrast check. The earlier benchmark's source read found a missing mobile menu below 820 px, border contrast around 1.4:1, and no reduced-motion guard; these came from an earlier version of the page, so re-check them against the current one.

The FAQ you already have is worth keeping for objection handling, but expect no search benefit. Google limited FAQ rich results to authoritative government and health sites in August 2023, and the advice is to leave FAQPage markup off marketing pages ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/faq-schema-rise-fall-seo-today-463993)). Native `<details>` elements keep JavaScript minimal.

## Local SEO forces one decision the team must make

Structured data and local search collide with having no confirmed address. Google's LocalBusiness markup requires `address` and `name` ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business)), while a business that serves customers at their location may run a service-area Google Business Profile with the address hidden, and Google's guideline puts service areas at about two hours' drive from the base ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en)). Hyderabad to Visakhapatnam is well beyond that, so a profile can realistically cover only one base metro. The notes' own judgment is that reaching the rest of two states needs real content per city, not doorway pages. If the founders will not publish an address, use Organization markup with `areaServed` for Andhra Pradesh and Telangana (schema.org defines `areaServed` as the area where a service is provided ([schema.org](https://schema.org/LocalBusiness))) and accept no local-pack markup. The notes did not verify rich-result eligibility without an address.

One correction to the earlier report: it suggested a Hindi or Hinglish line near the CTA. Novaframe's languages are Telugu, English and Tinglish, and the Think with Google guidance favours transliterated Latin-script text mainly for typing convenience. Hindi should be dropped from the recommendations.

## Conclusion

The sharpest finding is how little of the gap is design. The page's structure, pricing and tone already match or exceed a sample of Indian agency sites that mostly hide prices, show unverifiable stats and write in English only. What separates Novaframe from standard is verifiable output and a channel that works, and both depend on the founders, not on the page. The honest tension is that Telugu is Novaframe's differentiator but, as built, sits in the part of the page search engines are least likely to credit.

The evidence also argues for restraint. Most conversion claims here are vendor-reported, single-test or old, so every change below should be treated as a sensible default to measure, not a guaranteed lift. A browser run of Lighthouse on the live page and a Search Console view after launch would settle the largest open questions in the notes: mobile performance on mid-range Android, which Telugu and English queries actually bring visitors, and whether the WhatsApp button is tapped.
